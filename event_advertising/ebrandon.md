Westman Naturalists - Shoveler Circling Season by Baldur

A couple years ago we found large groups of Northern Shovelers engaged in a circling manoeuvre in the potholes and lakes north of Baldur. The circling stirs up the water and causes aquatic bugs/vegetation to rise to the surface, making for a nice meal! We're hoping to see this behaviour again! Lots of ducks and other migrants should be moving through the area at this time of year too. Also, it's [October Big Day](https://ebird.org/news/october-big-day-2026)!

The lakes are just over an hour southwest of Brandon. We'll be out there for a while, so consider bring snacks and/or a lunch. This outing will mostly be driving, stopping along the side of gravel roads to check for birds and other wildlife. Be sure to dress for the autumn weather. Bring your binoculars and/or spotting scope if you have them, we'll have a couple of extra scopes to share.

Saturday, Oct 17 2026 - 9:00 AM


Westman Naturalists - Waterfowl Around Rivers and Wahtopanah

Join us for a drive around Lake Wahtopanah by Rivers. We'll be stopping at a few good spots around the lake looking for migrating waterfowl, raptors, and songbirds. If we're lucky maybe we'll even see a cool Muskrat.

Minimizing the number of vehicles is always a good goal, if you're up for carpooling around the lake. Bring any binoculars/cameras/scopes you may have as some of the birds will be far out on the water! Check the forecast before coming out and dress appropriately for potential short walks along the shore. The optional walking can be a little rough with large stones/rocks, and maybe some mud, so boots are a good idea.

Westman Naturalists - [Talk] The early evolution of vertebrate life on land: how the world we know today came to be

Join Dr. Mark MacDougal of Brandon University for an engaging look at life's first steps onto land. Exploring early tetrapods, four-limbed vertebrates from roughly 290 million years ago, Mark will discuss how their evolution shaped vertebrate diversity and the ecosystems we know today. The talk highlights key German fossils from past research, plus current work on Canadian fossils expanding our understanding of vertebrate evolution.

This is a hybrid event! Feel free to come in person to meet Mark and see the talk, or join us via Zoom.
